The Green Commute Pulse of Melbourne

An interactive exploration of how Victorian infrastructure and daily habits intersect across our rail networks and active cycling corridors.

Section A: Melbourne's Transit Backbone at a Glance

Melbourne’s layout forces a choice for the green commuter: steel rails or two wheels? The map below reveals that while our rail layout spreads radially to connect the suburbs, the active cycling network relies heavily on disjointed trails. By finding the geometric intersection points, we can see exactly where multi-modal travel thrives—and where major transit stations completely lack safe, separated bike lanes.

Section B: The Pulse of the Week: Workdays vs Weekends

Our networks don't sleep, but their usage changes completely. Linking train station entries across time bands with hourly bicycle counters visualizes the rhythmic pulse of Melbourne's week. Train usage spikes symmetrically into tight morning and evening peaks, whereas cycling volumes remain flatter. On weekends, rail patronage drops dramatically, while cycling volumes climb as Melburnians pivot from professional commuting to leisure.

Section C: Under the Hood of Cycling Infrastructure

Paint on a road isn't a bike lane. Does better infrastructure actually attract more riders? Correlating physical path classifications against counter sites shows a clear trend: safe, separated off-road trails attract higher, steadier rider volumes. Meanwhile, on-road lanes see unpredictable speeds and lower usage, highlighting the safety concerns of mixing bicycles with vehicle traffic.

Section D: Geographic Flows and Seasonal Shifts

Where do we come from, and how does the weather change our habits? Grouping station patronage geographically tracks the massive morning migration from the suburbs into the CBD. Tracking this across a full calendar matrix reveals how Melbourne's cold, wet winter months cause a sharp drop in active cycling, while train passenger volumes remain steady year-round.

How does your local line stack up? This final dashboard compares daily passenger load against local bike access and targets, showing where our infrastructure meets modern demands.
